## The plan

**Serves:** learning

**Size:** L (5)

**What:** the rest of the guide: cleaning text and regex work;
counting and grouping, with sets, bags and the list utilities; shaping
output as JSON, YAML and dumps; making it faster, with `Linear` and
locking; and growing out of scalar, replacing it with plain Go once the
shape is known.

**Why:** these are the tasks scalar's users bring after reading their
data, and the last says plainly where scalar stops being the right
tool.

**Steps:**

1. Add recipes to the four chapters 2026-0010 made, each an example
   with a plain Go twin printing the same, generated by `TestHowTo`:
   - **Working with text:** a substitution with captures (`S` with
     `$1`); repeated words, found with a backreference, which Go's
     `regexp` hasn't got; and a template filled from a hash
     (`Interpolate`).
   - **Counting and grouping:** grouping by a field (`PartitionBy`);
     the strongest few, sorted by a field (`RevSortByNum`); and which
     stations were expected and silent (`Set`'s `Intersection` and
     `Difference`).
   - **Writing it out:** YAML (`ToYAML`), beside the JSON recipe.
   - **Making it faster:** a structure shared between goroutines for
     reading, locked first (`Lock`).
2. A chapter of its own, **Growing out of scalar**, before "Compared
   with plain Go": when to replace scalar with plain Go, and how, step
   by step, with what each step gives up. It draws on the twins, and
   needs no recipe of its own: the twins are the plain Go it moves to.
   Dumps (`Dump`) stay in the reference: they're for looking, and
   there's no plain Go twin that prints the same.
3. The guide's contents list the new recipes and chapter.
4. A CHANGELOG line, the record finished, and the guide's PDF read.

**Done when** each chapter's recipes are generated and checked as
2026-0008 sets out.
