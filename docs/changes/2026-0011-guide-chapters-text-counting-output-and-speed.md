## The plan

**Serves:** learning

**Size:** L (5)

**What:** the rest of the guide: cleaning text and regex work;
counting and grouping, with sets, bags and the list utilities; shaping
output as JSON, YAML and dumps; making it faster, with `Linear` and
locking; and growing out of scalar, replacing it with plain Go once the
shape is known.

**Why:** these are the tasks scalar's users bring after reading their
data, and the last says plainly where scalar stops being the right
tool.

**Steps:**

1. Add recipes to the four chapters 2026-0010 made, each an example
   with a plain Go twin printing the same, generated by `TestHowTo`:
   - **Working with text:** a substitution with captures (`S` with
     `$1`); repeated words, found with a backreference, which Go's
     `regexp` hasn't got; and a template filled from a hash
     (`Interpolate`).
   - **Counting and grouping:** grouping by a field (`PartitionBy`);
     the strongest few, sorted by a field (`RevSortByNum`); and which
     stations were expected and silent (`Set`'s `Intersection` and
     `Difference`).
   - **Writing it out:** YAML (`ToYAML`), beside the JSON recipe.
   - **Making it faster:** a structure shared between goroutines for
     reading, locked first (`Lock`).
2. A chapter of its own, **Growing out of scalar**, before "Compared
   with plain Go": when to replace scalar with plain Go, and how, step
   by step, with what each step gives up. It draws on the twins, and
   needs no recipe of its own: the twins are the plain Go it moves to.
   Dumps (`Dump`) stay in the reference: they're for looking, and
   there's no plain Go twin that prints the same.
3. The guide's contents list the new recipes and chapter.
4. A CHANGELOG line, the record finished, and the guide's PDF read.

**Done when** each chapter's recipes are generated and checked as
2026-0008 sets out.

## What happened

- **Outcome:** done as planned. Eight new recipes, each with a plain Go
  twin printing the same, took the guide to twenty-five, in ten
  chapters, before one was dropped (below):
  - Working with text: rewriting with captures; repeated words, by
    backreference; filling in a template.
  - Counting and grouping: grouping by a field; the strongest few;
    expected, and silent.
  - Writing it out: writing YAML.
  - Making it faster: sharing data between goroutines, which passes
    under `-race`, later dropped (below).

  "Growing out of scalar" is the new chapter: four steps from scalar to
  plain Go, each keeping the program working, pointing at the twins as
  the code each step moves to.
- **Found on the way:**
  - Two recipes first failed on Perl's rule that lists flatten:
    `Array(Hash(…))` spread each hash into its pairs, and a Go slice
    given to `Hash` spread too. Both are documented, and the reference
    has them; the YAML recipe now says so, wrapping what's nested in
    `scalar.Value`, and the template recipe reads its data from JSON,
    as it would arrive.
  - The first draft of the guide said a hash's keys come in the order
    they were added; the reference says, as in Perl, that the order isn't
    fixed, and the guide says that now.
  - The first draft of "Growing out of scalar" said a pattern `Linear`
    can run gives Go's `regexp` the same answers; the reference's What
    `Linear` translates shows it doesn't, for `\w`, `\d`, `\s` and named
    groups, and the chapter says so.
  - A probe confirmed scalar reads `$1x` in a replacement as `$1` then
    `x`, as Perl does, where Go's `regexp` reads a group named `1x`.
- **Changes from the plan:**
  - The template recipe reads JSON rather than building hashes, for the
    reason above; and reading the PDF moved three comments that wrapped
    or repeated the prose.
  - The goroutines recipe was dropped after Iain's review, 2026-11-09.
    Its plain Go, a map nothing writes, was as short and needed no lock,
    and it showed as the way to share data what the reference's
    Concurrency section gives as the fallback: build shared tables as
    Go maps, keep each scalar in one goroutine, and lock only what must
    stay in scalar. "Making it faster" ends with a caution saying that
    instead, with no recipe, as there's nothing for scalar to win; the
    guide has twenty-four recipes.
- **What's left:** nothing for the guide's chapters. The general part of
  the generator moves to docref when grammar's guide follows
  (2026-0008's D2 and D4).
